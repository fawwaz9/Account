# Jobs

> The build log, including what each stage's plan got wrong, is at
> [../jobs-plan.md](../jobs-plan.md). This is the standing design note.

## Context

One or several jobs, and the questions they raise: *when am I working*, *what am
I earning*, *did the cheque match the hours*, *is the job crowding my exams*,
and at year end *what do I tell the tax return*.

Deliberately two-faced. **Time** goes to the Calendar, so a shift competes with
lectures for the same hours and the existing free-block machinery sees it.
**Money** goes to the Budget only at the point a cheque arrives, and only the
net — every rate, gross figure and analysis lives here, because this is the only
place that can compute them.

**Decisions taken:** shifts are scheduled then confirmed, and an unconfirmed past
shift counts at its scheduled hours and says so; a job is hourly, salaried or
contract, each with its own expected-pay rule; a paycheque records gross and
named deductions and sends only the net, split by income share; a shift is a new
`EventKind` on the existing events table; tips are marked cash or on-cheque and
only cash may reach the ledger, into the one account chosen for cash tips.

## What it reuses, and what that bought

| Existing | What it gave |
|---|---|
| the recurrence engine | "every Tue and Thu" as the engine's fourth consumer, free |
| `free_blocks` | work competes with study, with no code that knows what work is |
| `CRUNCH_WEIGHTS.get(kind, 0)` | a shift scores zero and drops out of a measure of academic pressure |
| blackout scoping | a reading break cannot cancel a shift, because blackouts touch course events only |
| `split_income` | the net behaves like any other deposit |
| `tuition_payments` | the structural precedent, near-mechanically reversed |
| `week_load` | hours against class time, once split by kind |
| `list_occurrences` | shifts near exams, read from the calendar's own expansion, so the two pages agree (D21) |

Four behaviours for the price of one column and one enum member. That table is
the argument for writing *What already exists* before designing anything.

## Data model

`jobs` — name, employer, `kind`, `hourly_rate` (hourly only), `period_amount`
(salaried only), `started_on`, `ended_on` (null while current), `archived`, and
the five shared `recur_*` columns plus `pay_starts_on` for the pay schedule.

Those `recur_*` columns are **the paycheque's, not the shifts'**. On a job form
an unlabelled repeat editor reads as though it governs when you work, so the
field says what it is: *does the paycheque repeat?* Safe-to-spend is what reads
it, to decide how long a balance has to last.
Nothing is ever materialised from that rule: it answers "when is the next cheque",
and a cheque exists only because one arrived and was entered.

A job holds **only the pay field its own kind uses**. Two answers to "what should
this have paid" with nothing to choose between them is worse than one missing
answer. That rule cannot live on `JobUpdate` — a patch changing only `kind`
cannot see the rate already stored — so `check_pay_fields` enforces it in the
service and clears whichever field the new kind does not use.

`events.job_id` — one nullable column beside `course_id`, plus `EventKind.SHIFT`.

`shift_records` — what actually happened on one date: worked times, `confirmed`,
`tip_amount`, `tip_is_cash`, `tip_transaction_id`, `rate_override`, notes. Keyed
by **`(event_id, on_date)`**, because a repeating shift is one row expanded on
read and there is nothing anywhere that represents "this Friday". A row exists
only once there is something to say about that date.

`paycheques` + `paycheque_deductions` — gross, period, `expected_gross`, and a
`group_id` rather than a transaction id, because the net splits across accounts
and a deposit is already several rows tied by that column.

## Two shapes of shift, and why the default is one at a time

Part-time work is rarely the same hours twice: a rota posted a week ahead,
different every week, sometimes two shifts in a day. So **a single shift on a
single date is the default path**, and the weekly pattern sits behind a
disclosure for the jobs that do repeat. A form offering only a pattern would be
asking for a regularity most student jobs do not have.

Both are events. A one-off simply has no rule on it, so the calendar, the
free-block finder and the earnings figures all see it without knowing it was
typed rather than generated.

> **The trap this opened.** `set_shift_schedule` replaces the previous schedule,
> and its delete matched every shift the job had. Once one-offs existed, saving a
> pattern would have deleted a fortnight of hand-entered shifts — silently, since
> they would simply stop appearing. The delete is now narrowed to rows carrying a
> rule, with a test that fails if it is widened again.

Removing a one-off deletes the event and anything recorded about it. A date
generated by a pattern has no row of its own, so cancelling one of those is a
skipped date on the rule, which the calendar already offers. `ShiftOccurrence`
carries `repeats` so the client knows which is which without guessing from
whether a rule happens to be present.

> **A second orphan, the same shape as the first two.** `remove_shift` used to
> bulk-delete the `ShiftRecordModel` directly, which never touched the ledger
> rows a confirmed cash tip had already written -- a tip splits by income share
> into several rows tied by `group_id`, exactly like `sync_cash_tip` already has
> to unpick, and the record only points at one of them. Deleting a confirmed
> shift with a tip left that money in the ledger explaining nothing, the same
> fault the orphaned tuition payment and paycheque were made of, one module
> later. Fixed by walking the group before the record goes; mutation-tested by
> reverting to the bulk delete.

Deleting a confirmed shift asks first, since it can take real money out of the
ledger. Deleting an unconfirmed one does not -- there is nothing yet to lose.

## The distinctions that carry the module

**Scheduled vs worked.** An unconfirmed past shift counts at its scheduled hours
and is flagged `assumed`. Dropping it would make a fortnight nobody did the admin
for read as a fortnight nobody worked; auto-confirming it would claim as fact
something never checked.

**Earned vs paid.** Earned comes from the shifts and is a prediction; paid comes
from cheques that arrived. Reported apart everywhere, because one combined figure
would hide a fortnight worked that nobody has paid for — the thing the page
exists to surface.

**Gross vs net.** Deductions are money earned that never entered an account.
Recording them as income would invent a deposit; dropping them would make the
gross unexplainable. They sit on the cheque; only the net reaches the ledger.

**Cash vs on-cheque tips.** A cash tip is money already in hand and writes its own
income row. A tip paid through payroll arrives inside the cheque's gross, so a
row of its own would count it twice.

**A cash tip goes to one account, whole** (2026-10-04). It used to split across
accounts by income share like a paycheque, which claimed it was in the bank when
it was in a pocket. Settings → Financial now has "Cash tips go to", and the tip
lands there as one row (`sync_cash_tip`, `file_cash_tip`).
- **With no account chosen**, or with the chosen one archived or deleted, a tip is
  **held**: recorded on the shift, kept out of the budget, `tip_in_ledger` false.
  A red "!" beside the confirmed shift says so and links to Settings →
  Financial. Logging your hours must not fail over an unrelated budget setting.
- **Choosing an account files every held tip** into it. Choosing has its own
  endpoint (`PUT /jobs/{user_id}/cash-tip-account`), not the generic
  preferences save, because it writes to the ledger and needs an ownership
  check in the same transaction.
- **Changing the account affects tips from then on.** A tip already in the budget
  is never moved by re-saving its shift, including old tips split under the
  previous rule.

**Expected, per kind.** Hourly compares hours × rate. Salaried compares the period
amount and treats hours as what the job *costs* you, never as evidence of
underpayment. Contract compares nothing, because you invoiced it. Converting a
salary to an implied hourly rate would make a long week read as a pay cut.

## The four figures

One service, one pass, in the shape of `load_service`:

- **Month / term**, per job and total. Term dates from `Grades/utils/terms.py`,
  the same accessor Term Budget uses. It once restated that rule here instead of
  importing it, and the two disagreed: the term began on the earliest course's
  start date while the budget's began on Sep 1, so "this term" and "this past
  week" reported the same figure.
  A **week** window is still computed and still on the wire, and is deliberately
  not drawn — pay does not arrive weekly, so a weekly pay figure was a number
  nobody could act on. It stays because `test_earnings.py` makes thirteen
  assertions through it, where a fixed seven days keeps the expected shift
  counts obvious; month-to-date holds a different number of shifts depending on
  the day. Removing it means recomputing every one of those, in tests whose
  subject is shift arithmetic rather than which cards render.
- **Effective hourly rate** — net ÷ hours, beside the advertised rate. Measured
  over the periods cheques actually *covered*, not every hour worked: otherwise
  unpaid hours drag it down and a cheque still to come reads as a pay cut.
- **Hours against class time** — from `week_load`'s `work_minutes` /
  `class_minutes` split.
- **Unpaid, and when** — hours since the last cheque's period, plus the next date
  the pay rule produces.

## Is this cheque right?

`check_paycheque` (`services/paycheck_service.py`) recomputes a cheque from its
shifts and shows the arithmetic line by line, each line naming the rule it came
from (D11). The rules are pure functions in `services/pay_rules.py`:
- **Overtime**, BC Employment Standards Act s.40. Time and a half over 8 hours a
  day up to 12, double time over 12. Time and a half over 40 a week (Sunday to
  Saturday), not counting hours already paid as daily overtime.
- **Vacation pay**, s.58: 4% of gross wages, 6% after five years. Tips are not
  wages for this.

**It cuts both ways**, because a false "you're short" erodes trust as fast as a
missed real one:
- Vacation pay is checked **both ways**. The app can't know whether an employer
  pays the 4% on each cheque or holds it, so a cheque matching either
  expectation matches. One between the two says to check the vacation-pay line.
- **Short is measured against the smaller expectation**, so a "short" flag never
  depends on that guess.
- The tolerance is a dollar, or 0.5% on a large cheque (`tolerance`).
  Rounding on a real cheque is cents, sometimes a dollar, never a percent.
- **What it can't see, it says.** Statutory holiday pay (s.44-45) is not
  checked: its eligibility test reaches outside the pay period.

This is what replaced the old "not built: overtime rules" below. The per-shift
rate override still covers a night differential or an employer's own rule.

## Shifts near exams

`conflict_service.find_conflicts` (D21). A shift **overlaps** an exam if they
share any time. It **crowds** one if it ends before the exam starts, but by less
than your buffer (`exam_buffer_hours`, Settings → Calendar, default 12, 0
flags overlaps only). That catches a closing shift the night before an 8:30
final.

It reads the calendar's own occurrences, so skipped dates, blackouts and
repeating shifts all agree with what the calendar shows. Jobs reading the
calendar is the allowed direction, and nothing here writes. **Untimed items flag
more, never less**: an exam with no time is taken to start at 08:00, the
earliest a UBC exam sits, and run the day. A shift with no times runs the whole
day. Shown on Jobs and on the Calendar, and silent when there is nothing.

## The tax packet

`tax_packet_service.tax_packet` (D20, D21): for a calendar year, recorded pay per
job and the tuition paid in it. It is built **only from what you recorded**:
paycheques, cash tips and tuition payments. A cheque counts in the year it
arrived (`occurred_on`), which is when employment income is taxed. A deduction
is sorted by its name (income tax, CPP, EI, or kept under its own name), because
that is all a recorded deduction says about itself.

**The app never reads a T4** (D20). The slip is the most personal document there
is, so T4 upload was scrapped and the page tells you to check its sums against
the slip instead. No personal document is ever sent to a model.

### Paid leads; the guesses live together

The window cards are **money that arrived**: net paid, the gross and deduction
behind it, and which jobs it came from. Everything the shifts merely predict —
hours worked, earned, cash tips, unconfirmed shifts, unpaid hours — sits under
**Estimates**, which is the old "Not paid yet" block renamed and widened rather
than a third section competing with it.

`JobEarnings` gained `gross_paid`/`deducted`/`net_paid` to make that possible:
the window carried a paid total from the start but no way to say which job it
came from, so a per-job breakdown could only ever show earned.

> **A job with a cheque and no shifts still belongs in the breakdown.** Pay
> arrives for work done before the window opened — that is the normal case. The
> per-job loop used to skip any job with no shifts and nothing earned, which
> dropped exactly those rows while the window's own total still counted the
> money, so the rows silently failed to add up to the headline above them.

The two figures are **not a sum and a remainder**, and putting them in separate
sections makes that easier to forget: earned counts shifts falling *inside* a
window, paid counts cheques *dated* inside it.

## Notes

- `expected_gross` is frozen when the cheque is recorded and never recomputed. A
  shift edited months later must not overturn a verdict already reached — the
  same reason tuition stores `credits_counted`.
- `record_deposit` commits on its own, so it cannot be reused: the rows and the
  cheque must land in one transaction. `deposit_rows` builds them unsaved from
  the same `split_income`.
- A cash tip filed today is one row. A tip from before 2026-10-04 is **several**,
  split by income share and tied by `group_id`. The record points at one and the
  group finds the rest, so anything removing a tip walks the group. Deleting only
  the row it points at orphans the others.
- `db.refresh()` reloads from the database and discards pending edits. It silently
  undid a corrected gross. `db.flush()` was what was wanted.
- Do not delete deductions by hand: the relationship's `delete-orphan` cascade
  does it, and doing both leaves SQLAlchemy warning on every commit. The suite
  runs with `-W error::sqlalchemy.exc.SAWarning` to catch the next one.
- Any service taking an `on_day` must thread it into everything it calls.
  `window_earnings` did not, so "past" meant the wall clock rather than the day
  being reported on.
- `SHIFT` must stay out of `CLASS_KINDS` — but that is not what protects shifts
  from a timetable rewrite; the `course_id` match does. Two tests, for two
  different things.

## Pay dates feed the money room

The forecast and the year plan (see [finances.md](finances.md)) read each current
job's pay dates from its rule. An hourly job is projected from its scheduled
shifts, and the pay is scaled by the share of income that lands in the accounts
being forecast. Jobs owns the schedule; Finances only reads it.

## Not built, deliberately

- **Statutory holiday pay** in the cheque check, for the reason above.
- **Invoices as their own record.** A contract cheque *is* the invoice being paid;
  there is nothing separate to track until one needs chasing.
- **T4 upload** (D20), scrapped for good.

Two items once listed here have since been built: BC overtime and vacation pay,
in the cheque check, and a single account for cash tips.

## Still open

Pay on specific days of the month is proposed in `next.md`: set dates, the last
day of the month, or the Nth weekday, with weekend handling chosen per job. A
monthly rule today can only repeat on the day of the month it started.
